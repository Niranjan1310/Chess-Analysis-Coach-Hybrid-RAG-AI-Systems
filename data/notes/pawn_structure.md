# Pawn Structure Fundamentals

Pawn structure is the skeleton of a chess position. Unlike pieces, pawns
cannot move backward, so every pawn move permanently alters the position.

## Doubled Pawns

Doubled pawns occur when two pawns of the same color are on the same file.
They are generally a weakness because:
- They cannot protect each other.
- The file in front of the rear pawn is half-open for the opponent.
- They reduce pawn mobility and limit pawn breaks.

However, doubled pawns can also open files for rooks and provide extra
control of key squares.

## Isolated Pawns

An isolated pawn has no friendly pawns on adjacent files. The square in
front of an isolated pawn is a natural outpost for enemy pieces because
no pawn can challenge a piece placed there.

The isolated queen's pawn (IQP) is a special case: it gives dynamic
chances (open files, piece activity) but becomes a weakness in endgames.

## Backward Pawns

A backward pawn is one that cannot advance because the square in front
is controlled by an enemy pawn and there are no friendly pawns behind
it on adjacent files. The square in front of a backward pawn is another
strong outpost for the opponent.
